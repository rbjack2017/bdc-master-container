Objections Module

The Objections Module teaches reps how to respond when customers push back, hesitate, or express concerns. Objection handling must be calm, helpful, compliant, and focused on keeping the conversation moving.

1. Purpose of Objection Handling

  • Reduce customer hesitation
  • Provide clarity and reassurance
  • Keep the conversation alive
  • Maintain compliance
  • Guide customers toward the next step
  • Prevent lead loss

2. Objection Handling Rules

Core Rules

  • Match your reply to the customer’s exact concern
  • Stay calm and professional
  • Never argue or contradict
  • Never pressure the customer
  • Never make guarantees
  • Always end with one clear next step

Compliance Rules

  • No pricing in text messages
  • No payment quotes
  • No approval guarantees
  • No misleading statements
  • No discussing credit details outside secure application

3. Common Objections & Approved Responses

“I’m just looking.”

Approved Response:

Totally fine — I can help make the search easier. Is there a specific model or price range you’re leaning toward?

“I’m not ready yet.”

Approved Response:

No problem — I can still help you compare options. What timeline are you thinking?

“I only want the price.”

Approved Response:

I can help with accurate pricing. To make sure it’s correct, could you confirm your ZIP code and whether you have a trade?

“Is it available?”

Approved Response:

I can check availability — are you looking for this exact trim or open to similar options?

“I don’t want to come in.”

Approved Response:

Totally fine — I can send photos, Carfax, or details. What would you like first?

“I’m worried about my credit.”

Approved Response:

We work with many lenders, and our Finance Team can review your information privately. I can send the secure application if you’d like.

“I already bought somewhere else.”

Approved Response:

Thanks for letting me know — if you ever need service or help comparing future options, I’m here anytime.

4. Objection Handling Structure

Every objection response follows a simple structure:

  1. Acknowledge the concern
  2. Reassure the customer
  3. Provide clarity or a helpful option
  4. Ask one low-effort question

5. Low-Effort Questions

Low-effort questions keep the conversation alive without overwhelming the customer.

  • “What matters most — price, mileage, or features?”
  • “Are you leaning toward new or pre-owned?”
  • “Would you prefer similar options or incoming units?”
  • “Do you have a trade?”

6. Tone Requirements

Objection handling must be friendly, calm, and helpful.

  • Warm tone
  • Short sentences
  • Clear next steps
  • No pressure
  • No negativity

7. Objection Handling Violations

Violations occur when reps respond incorrectly or break compliance.

Minor Violations

  • Overly long responses
  • Missing next step
  • Not addressing the customer’s concern

Major Violations

  • Guarantees
  • Pricing in text messages
  • Arguing with the customer
  • Pressure tactics

8. Objection Summary

Objection handling keeps conversations alive, reduces hesitation, and guides customers toward the next step. When done correctly, objections become opportunities.
